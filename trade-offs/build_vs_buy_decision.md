# Build vs Buy Decision

## 1. Introduction

Build vs Buy is a strategic decision that determines whether an organization should develop a software solution internally or purchase an existing product.

The decision affects development time, cost, customization, maintenance, and long-term flexibility.

## 2. What Does Build Mean?

Building means developing a solution using internal engineering resources.

Examples:
- Developing an authentication system.
- Creating a custom monitoring platform.
- Building an internal deployment tool.
- Developing a custom payment workflow.

## 3. What Does Buy Mean?

Buying means adopting an existing commercial or open-source product instead of developing the complete solution internally.

Examples:
- Using a managed authentication platform.
- Purchasing a monitoring service.
- Using an existing CRM product.
- Adopting a commercial CI/CD platform.

## 4. Comparison

| Factor | Build | Buy |
|---|---|---|
| Development time | Usually longer | Usually shorter |
| Customization | High | Depends on product |
| Initial cost | Engineering investment | Licensing or subscription |
| Maintenance | Internal responsibility | Shared or vendor-managed |
| Control | Greater | Limited by product |
| Vendor dependency | Lower | Potentially higher |

## 5. Decision Factors

### Business Requirements

Determine whether existing products satisfy the required functionality.

### Development Cost

Estimate engineering time, infrastructure, testing, and maintenance.

### Time to Market

Evaluate whether developing internally delays important business objectives.

### Customization

Determine whether the organization needs unique workflows.

### Security and Compliance

Check whether available products satisfy regulatory and security requirements.

### Long-Term Ownership

Consider support, upgrades, migration, and vendor lock-in.

## 6. Decision Matrix

| Requirement | Build | Buy |
|---|---|---|
| Unique business logic | Strong choice | May be limited |
| Standard functionality | Often unnecessary | Strong choice |
| Fast implementation | Slower | Faster |
| Full control | Strong choice | Limited |
| Limited engineering team | Difficult | Often suitable |

## 7. Practical Example

An organization needs an employee management platform.

It can:
- Build a custom application to support unique workflows.
- Purchase an existing HR management solution.
- Combine both approaches by integrating a commercial product with custom services.

## 8. Best Practices

- Identify core business capabilities.
- Compare total cost of ownership.
- Evaluate vendor reliability.
- Review licensing conditions.
- Assess security requirements.
- Consider future scalability.
- Run a proof of concept before committing.

## 9. Benefits of the Right Decision

- Better resource allocation.
- Reduced unnecessary development.
- Faster delivery.
- Improved operational efficiency.
- Better alignment with business goals.

## 10. Conclusion

Build vs Buy decisions should be based on business value, cost, customization, security, and long-term maintenance. Neither approach is universally better.
